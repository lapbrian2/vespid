Thorax

Pure flight muscle

Three fused segments house the indirect flight muscles that power both wing pairs. The thorax is the engine that makes a wasp airborne in milliseconds.

[03]
Petiole

The wasp waist

The narrow petiole connecting thorax to abdomen isn't just aesthetic — it allows the abdomen to curl underneath for stinging. Form follows function.

[04]
Stinger

Smooth. Reusable.

Unlike a bee's barbed stinger, a wasp's ovipositor is smooth — it can sting repeatedly without self-destructing. The venom is alkaline, designed to paralyze prey, not just warn predators.

[05]
Compound Eyes

You were seen first

Nearly 360-degree vision from thousands of hexagonal lenses. Wasps detect ultraviolet patterns invisible to humans. They track movement faster than any vertebrate eye.

[06]
Wings

40 beats per second

Two pairs of wings, hooked together mid-flight by tiny hamuli. Forewings generate lift, hindwings stabilize. At rest, they fold flat along the body — ready to deploy in an instant.
