Vespa orientalis

The Oriental Hornet

The only known insect that harvests solar energy. A yellow stripe on its abdomen contains xanthopterin — a pigment that converts light into electricity.

Thorax

Pure flight muscle

Three fused segments. Two wing pairs. Forty beats per second. The thorax is the engine that makes a wasp airborne in milliseconds.

Stinger

Smooth. Reusable.

Unlike a bee, a wasp's stinger is smooth. It can sting repeatedly without self-destructing. The venom is alkaline — designed to paralyze, not warn.

Vision

You were seen first

Thousands of hexagonal lenses give nearly 360-degree vision. They detect ultraviolet patterns invisible to us. They track movement faster than any vertebrate eye.

Anatomy

Built to hunt

Mandibles for cutting. A petiole that allows the abdomen to curl under for stinging. Six legs tipped with claws. Every part serves the predator.
